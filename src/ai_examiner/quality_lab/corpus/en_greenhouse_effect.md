title: The Greenhouse Effect
---
The greenhouse effect is the process by which certain gases in Earth's atmosphere trap heat. Sunlight, mostly in visible and near-infrared wavelengths, passes through the atmosphere and warms the surface. The warmed surface emits energy back as longer-wavelength infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases such as water vapour, carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide absorb part of this infrared radiation and re-emit it in all directions, including back toward the surface.

Without any natural greenhouse effect, Earth's average surface temperature would be roughly minus 18 degrees Celsius instead of about 15 degrees Celsius. The natural greenhouse effect is therefore essential for life. The concern today is the enhanced greenhouse effect: human activities, mainly burning fossil fuels and deforestation, have raised the atmospheric concentration of carbon dioxide from about 280 parts per million before the industrial era to more than 420 parts per million.

Water vapour is the most abundant greenhouse gas, but its concentration mostly responds to temperature rather than driving it directly; it acts as a feedback that amplifies warming caused by other gases. Methane traps much more heat per molecule than carbon dioxide but stays in the atmosphere for a shorter time.

A common misconception is that the greenhouse effect and the ozone hole are the same problem. Ozone depletion lets more ultraviolet radiation reach the surface, and it was mainly caused by chlorofluorocarbons; it is a different mechanism from greenhouse warming.
